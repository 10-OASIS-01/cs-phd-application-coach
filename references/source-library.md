## Start with these application guides

- [Student Perspectives on Applying to NLP PhD Programs](https://blog.nelsonliu.me/2019/10/24/student-perspectives-on-applying-to-nlp-phd-programs/) — broad applicant perspectives on research experience, program lists, SOPs, letters, interviews, and choosing offers. Use for durable patterns, not current requirements.
- [Inside Ph.D. Admissions](https://nschneid.medium.com/inside-ph-d-admissions-what-readers-look-for-in-a-statement-of-purpose-3db4e6081f80) by Nathan Schneider — organizes SOP reading around qualifications, focus, and fit. Useful for evidence-based review.
- [All About PhD Applications](https://lucylai.com/blog/gradapps) by Lucy Lai — detailed planning, fee, statement, and interview guidance with examples; distinguish neuroscience-specific material.
- [Machine Learning PhD Applications](https://timdettmers.com/2018/11/26/phd-applications/) by Tim Dettmers — extensive personal guidance on research, letters, schools, and materials. Treat probability estimates, prestige claims, and administrative details as historical opinion rather than policy.
- [Applying to PhD Programs in Computer Science](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~harchol/gradschooltalk.pdf) by Mor Harchol-Balter — a classic process overview with useful statement and recommender-packet ideas; dated tests, rankings, and funding specifics require verification.
- [The Hidden Curriculum of CS PhD Applications](https://10-oasis-01.github.io/blog/the-hidden-curriculum-of-cs-phd-applications/) — the public guide from which this skill's access-conscious framing and resource map were developed.

## Hidden curriculum, access, and recommendations

- [CS PhD FAQ](https://jessethomason.com/faq) by Jesse Thomason — explicitly discusses access to unwritten rules, networking, fee waivers, and making work visible.
- [Talking to Robots FAQ](https://talkingtorobots.com/FAQ.html) by Yonatan Bisk — useful counterweight to blanket “never email faculty” advice; emphasizes that insiders may already be known while outsiders need thoughtful ways to introduce relevant work. Do not convert this into mass-email advice.
- [Advice to Graduate School Recommendation Letter Writers](https://cs.brown.edu/~sk/Memos/Grad-School-Recos/) by Shriram Krishnamurthi — explains why firsthand, concrete, calibrated evidence is more informative than generic praise.
- [Queer in AI Grad App Aid](https://www.queerinai.com/grad-app-aid), [Black in AI](https://www.blackinai.org/), [LatinX in AI Mentorship](https://www.latinxinai.org/mentorship-program), and [AccessComputing](https://accesscomputing.uw.edu/) — current-cycle discovery points for community, review, and access support. Recheck active cohorts and eligibility.
- [CS PhD Application Fee Waivers](https://github.com/KaiserWhoLearns/CS-PhD-Application-fee-waivers) — community discovery list whose entries can become stale.
- [GEM Fellowship](https://www.gemfellowship.org/gem-fellowship-program/) — official program page; verify citizenship, member-university, employer, internship, and funding terms.

## Statements and example materials

- [CS-SOP](https://cs-sop.org/) — a collection of statement examples. Use for genre comparison only: examples are not representative of all successful applicants and language must not be copied.
- [Siddhartha Gairola's ELLIS motivation letter](https://sidgairo18.github.io/grad_school_resources/Siddhartha_Gairola_Motivation_Letter_ELLIS.pdf) — an evidence-rich example for structural analysis, not a template to imitate sentence by sentence.
- [Academic CV template](https://www.overleaf.com/read/zmbjkwtfgpzf#9c9e77) — optional presentation starting point; content and accessibility matter more than the template.
- [Academic homepage template](https://github.com/10-OASIS-01/10-OASIS-01.github.io) — optional public artifact index for work, code, talks, and contact information.

## Interviews, PhD life, and careers

- [A Survival Guide to a PhD](https://karpathy.github.io/2016/09/07/phd/) by Andrej Karpathy — useful reflections on advisor and lab fit, research iteration, and varied paths; culturally dated in places and not a wellbeing rulebook.
- [How to Be a Successful PhD Student](https://www.cs.jhu.edu/~mdredze/publications/HowtoBeaSuccessfulPhDStudent.1_1.pdf) by Mark Dredze and Hanna Wallach — research habits, writing, collaboration, organization, and life outside work; some assumptions reflect its era.
- [How to Interview](https://www.cs.jhu.edu/~jason/advice/how-to-interview.html) and [Jason Eisner's advice collection](https://www.cs.jhu.edu/~jason/advice/) — research-conversation preparation and mutual evaluation.
- [Lessons from My PhD](https://tomsilver.github.io/blog/2024/lessons/) by Tom Silver — health, collaboration, progress, research styles, and the reminder that no single archetype defines a good researcher.
- [Academia to Industry](https://rowanmcallister.github.io/post/industry/) by Rowan McAllister — useful for career optionality, networking, rejection, and industry research transitions.

## Planning and presentation tools

- [PhD Application OS](https://app.notion.com/p/PhD-Application-OS-3b9e84e3538a8127a907f430035c5bcf?source=copy_link) — a Notion system for outreach, deadlines, materials, document versions, and status. The portable workspace in this skill separates programs, faculty, materials, and letters more rigorously and adds source freshness.
- [Open CS Application](https://opencs.app/) — Chinese-language community knowledge, much of it oriented toward North American master's applications. Do not use as authoritative PhD policy.
- [Global CS Application](https://global-cs-application.github.io/) — community exploration of European, Hong Kong, and Singapore programs, with substantial master's emphasis. Verify doctoral opportunities independently.

## Field-specific research exploration

These are optional on-ramps, never admissions prerequisites.

### Language models and planning

- [AAAI 2024 tutorial: On the Role of Large Language Models in Planning](https://yochan-lab.github.io/tutorial/AAAI-2024/index.html) — critical treatment of LLM planning and integration with planners; replaces the fragile tokenized Dropbox link.
- [Reward Models](https://rlhfbook.com/c/05-reward-models) in Nathan Lambert's RLHF book — reward-modeling foundations. Chapter numbering and URL can change; verify navigation.
- [Alisa's Book of LLMs](https://alisawuffles.notion.site/alisa-s-book-of-llms) and [math notes](https://alisawuffles.notion.site/math-notes) — potentially useful Notion resources that may require access or become unavailable.
- [Exploring Language Models](https://newsletter.maartengrootendorst.com/) by Maarten Grootendorst — visual explanations and current topic exploration; use as learning material, not an admissions authority.

### Robotics and planning

- [Integrated Task and Motion Planning](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-control-091420-084139) — canonical Annual Reviews article; replaces an expiring tokenized PDF URL.
- [Bilevel Planning for Robots: An Illustrated Introduction](https://lis.csail.mit.edu/bilevel-planning-for-robots-an-illustrated-introduction/) — accessible explanation of discrete-continuous robot planning.
- [NVIDIA Physical AI learning path](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/learn/learning-path/robotics/) — vendor-provided robotics learning path; product details and availability can change.

### Graphics and spatial mathematics

- [Fundamentals of Computer Graphics](https://www.routledge.com/Fundamentals-of-Computer-Graphics/Shirley-Marschner/p/book/9781003050339) — official publisher page; replaces an unauthorized PDF mirror.
- [3D Math Primer for Graphics and Game Development](https://gamemath.com/book/intro.html) — free online mathematics resource.
- [RCFS](https://rcfs.ch/#about) — interactive spatial or graphics learning resource; verify availability and scope before recommending a specific module.

### Research methods and cognition

- [Grounded theory introduction](https://delvetool.com/blog/groundedtheory) by Delve — practical qualitative-method overview from a commercial tool; pair with scholarly methods sources for serious study design.
- Lawrence Barsalou's [Grounded Cognition](https://www.barsaloulab.org/Online_Articles/2008-Barsalou-ARP-grounded_cognition.pdf) — foundational review for grounded and embodied cognition.

## Use and exclusion rules

- Synthesize and link; do not redistribute full copyrighted sources or sample essays.
- Prefer canonical articles, official publisher pages, and durable project pages over tokenized file links or mirrors.
- Mark older advice as historical experience where culture, tests, funding, or administrative rules may have changed.
- Keep field-specific resources out of applicant requirement checklists.
- When sources disagree, preserve the disagreement and explain which admission model or applicant context makes each view more applicable.
- Exclude redundant resources only after recording the durable insight elsewhere; exclude pirated, expired, unverifiable, or irrelevant links explicitly rather than silently treating them as evidence.
